Figure 2 broad-sense heritability (H2) summary

For each protein, replicate abundance measurements were transformed using the
protein-specific Box-Cox lambda used to generate the QTL `BC_DATA` values and
modeled with a random-intercept linear mixed model:

    abundance_ij = mu + strain_i + residual_ij

The strain effect was treated as a random effect and models were fitted by
restricted maximum likelihood. Broad-sense heritability was estimated as the
fraction of total phenotypic variance attributable to between-strain
variation:

    H2 = sigma_strain^2 / (sigma_strain^2 + sigma_residual^2)

This estimate captures stable genetic-background-associated strain differences
and is distinct from narrow-sense heritability, which refers specifically to
additive genetic variance. The forest plot intervals are 95% percentile
intervals from parametric bootstrap simulations of the fitted mixed model.
The displayed Pvalue is Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) adjusted across the 26 protein tests. The
unadjusted test is from a likelihood-ratio comparison of the random-
intercept model fitted by maximum likelihood with an intercept-only model; it
is a descriptive test of whether the strain variance component improves fit.

Protein expression profiles use the per-protein Box-Cox values (`BC_DATA`)
already supplied to the QTL analyses. Each protein is then z-scored across
strains for the heatmap and clustering. No pseudocount or log2 transformation
is applied. Strains are ordered by the gap-statistic cluster assignments.

For the mixed model, replicate measurements that were zero or otherwise
undefined under the protein-specific Box-Cox transform were omitted; they were
not replaced with a pseudocount.
